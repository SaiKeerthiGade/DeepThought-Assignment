# Learnability Section: ROAS, ACOS, and TACOS

## Concept 1: ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)

### What it Measures

ROAS tells us how much revenue is generated for every rupee spent on advertising.

### Formula

ROAS = Ad Revenue ÷ Ad Spend

### Example

If a brand spends ₹1,00,000 on Amazon ads and generates ₹4,00,000 in sales from those ads:

ROAS = 4,00,000 ÷ 1,00,000 = 4

This means every ₹1 spent on advertising generates ₹4 in revenue.

### Why a Brand Spending ₹15L/Month Cares

A brand spending ₹15 lakh per month needs to know whether the advertising investment is producing enough sales. A low ROAS means advertising is becoming inefficient and may reduce profitability.

### What is a Good ROAS?

There is no universal answer.

* Beauty/Skincare: Often 3–5+
* Electronics: Sometimes 2–3 is acceptable due to lower margins
* Premium products: Usually need higher ROAS because customer acquisition costs are higher

A good ROAS depends on product margins, competition, and growth goals.

## Concept 2: ACOS (Advertising Cost of Sale)

### What it Measures

ACOS shows what percentage of ad-generated revenue is spent on advertising.

### Formula

ACOS = Ad Spend ÷ Ad Revenue × 100

### Example

Ad Spend = ₹1,00,000
Ad Revenue = ₹4,00,000

ACOS = 25%

This means 25% of revenue generated from ads was spent on advertising.

### Relationship Between ACOS and ROAS

ROAS = Revenue ÷ Spend

ACOS = Spend ÷ Revenue

They are mathematical inverses.

Examples:

ROAS = 4 → ACOS = 25%

ROAS = 5 → ACOS = 20%

ROAS = 2 → ACOS = 50%

Higher ROAS means lower ACOS.

### When High ACOS is Acceptable

A brand may tolerate high ACOS when:

* Launching a new product
* Entering a competitive category
* Trying to improve keyword rankings
* Acquiring new customers

### When High ACOS Becomes a Problem

* Profit margins become negative
* Ad costs exceed gross profit
* No growth in organic sales accompanies the spending

## Concept 3: TACOS (Total Advertising Cost of Sale)

### What it Measures

TACOS measures advertising spend against total revenue, including both ad-driven sales and organic sales.

### Formula

TACOS = Ad Spend ÷ Total Revenue × 100

### Example

Ad Spend = ₹1,00,000

Ad Revenue = ₹4,00,000

Organic Revenue = ₹6,00,000

Total Revenue = ₹10,00,000

TACOS = 10%

### Why TACOS is Different from ACOS

ACOS only measures ad-attributed sales.

TACOS measures the health of the entire business.

A campaign may have a high ACOS but still be healthy if it helps increase organic sales.

### Why TACOS is Better for Long-Term Decisions

Advertising should not only generate immediate sales.

It should:

* Improve product ranking
* Increase brand awareness
* Generate reviews
* Drive future organic sales

TACOS captures these effects better than ACOS.

### What Happens When Organic Sales Grow?

Example:

Month 1:
Ad Spend = ₹1,00,000
Total Revenue = ₹5,00,000

TACOS = 20%

Month 6:
Ad Spend = ₹1,00,000
Total Revenue = ₹10,00,000

TACOS = 10%

Advertising spend stayed the same, but organic growth increased total revenue. This causes TACOS to decline, showing that the business is becoming more efficient.

---

## Connecting ROAS, ACOS, and TACOS

ROAS tells us how effective ads are.

ACOS tells us how much of ad revenue is consumed by advertising costs.

TACOS tells us whether advertising is helping the overall business grow.

A good Amazon analyst should not focus only on ROAS or ACOS. The real goal is sustainable growth, and TACOS helps measure that over time.
